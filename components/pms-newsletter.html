From the desk

Boring months are where alpha is decided

The headline reads like a bad month: benchmarks slipped, foreign money kept leaving, and the monsoon forecast added a layer of nerves. But a falling index is a poor reason to change a plan — and often a decent time to build one. The Nifty's P/E has drifted below its long-run average, and every prior instance of that over the last two decades was followed by positive returns one, two and three years out. That is not a promise. It is a reminder that the entry points people wish for in hindsight rarely feel comfortable in the moment.

Underneath the index, the dispersion was enormous. Small- and mid-caps quietly outran large-caps for the month, and the gap between the best and worst PMS in the same category ran to double digits. That gap is the whole argument for choosing a manager on process rather than on last quarter's chart. This brief deliberately does not list all 1,734 strategies — a wall of numbers flatters nobody. We show the funds that beat their benchmark, and we show a few that didn't, because both are the point.

One request we keep making: judge a PMS on the 3-year and 5-year column before the 1-month one. Mean reversion in this space is brutal and impartial — it drags last year's winners back and lifts the unfashionable. The funds worth your ₹50 lakh are the ones that beat the benchmark across cycles, not the ones having a good four weeks.
