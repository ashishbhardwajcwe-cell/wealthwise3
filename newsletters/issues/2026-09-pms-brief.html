From the desk

September was a red month for most portfolios: 1,359 of the 1,663 strategies with a monthly figure — more than four in five — closed it lower. Months like this invite the same reflex every time: move money toward whatever held up. We’d argue for the opposite habit. One month is noise, and even one year is a weak signal.

Three years is where skill starts to separate from luck, so that is the window this brief is built around. Over it, most strategies with a full record trailed a plain index. These figures are already net of each manager’s fees and expenses — SEBI requires portfolio managers to report them that way — so this is roughly what investors actually kept. The few that beat the index in every window we measure are listed below, followed by a pair of strategies that make the same point from the other direction.

Over three years, 686 of 1,019 strategies with a full record returned less than the S&P BSE 500 TRI, which compounded at 12.1% a year. The typical strategy — the median — managed 9.6%: about 2.5 percentage points a year behind the index, after fees.

Less than half beat the index over a single year, a third did it over three, and about one in five managed it in every window. The longer and stricter the test, the shorter the list. That is the pattern this brief keeps returning to, and it is why we rank on alpha rather than on last month’s chart.

A weak year inside a weak record

A weak year is not, on its own, a reason to leave a strategy, and a familiar name is not, on its own, a reason to stay. The multi-year record against the index is the more useful signal — and because these returns are already after fees, it shows what the manager actually delivered over a plain index you could own for a fraction of the cost.
